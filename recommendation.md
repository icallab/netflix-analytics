# Netflix Analytics Dashboard
## Business Recommendations

### Purpose

The recommendations below are derived from the catalog patterns identified in the Netflix dataset. They are intended as **areas for further evaluation**, not claims about Netflix's actual internal strategy or guaranteed business outcomes.

Because the dataset contains catalog metadata rather than viewing, revenue, subscriber, or cost information, each recommendation identifies additional data that would be needed to assess its business impact.

---

## 1. Evaluate Opportunities for International Content Diversification

**Supporting Findings**

- The catalog contains titles associated with 122 countries.
- The United States contributes 3,690 titles.
- India contributes 1,046 titles, followed by the United Kingdom with 806 titles.

**Recommendation**

Evaluate opportunities to broaden the geographic representation of the content catalog, including further exploration of Asian, African, and Latin American markets. Country-level opportunities should be assessed individually rather than assuming that a high title count automatically indicates strong audience demand.

**Additional Data Required**

- Subscribers by country or region.
- Viewing hours and completion rates by country.
- Content acquisition and production costs.
- Regional audience engagement.
- Licensing availability and market conditions.

**Potential Business Value**

A more geographically diversified catalog may provide additional content options across markets. Its effect on subscriber growth or retention would need to be measured using audience and subscription data.

---

## 2. Review Genre Representation and Content Gaps

**Supporting Findings**

- International Movies is the largest genre category, with 2,752 titles.
- Dramas is the second-largest category, with 2,427 titles.
- Comedies and International TV Shows are also among the most represented categories.

**Recommendation**

Use the current genre distribution as a baseline for reviewing the balance of the catalog. Identify genres with substantial representation and genres with comparatively fewer titles. Any decision to increase or reduce a genre should also consider audience demand and content performance.

**Additional Data Required**

- Viewing hours by genre.
- Completion rates by genre.
- Subscriber engagement by genre.
- Production and acquisition costs by genre.
- Genre performance across different regions.

**Potential Business Value**

A combination of catalog representation and audience performance data can help identify content gaps and support more evidence-based genre planning.

---

## 3. Evaluate Family and Kids Content Opportunities

**Supporting Findings**

- Little Kids represents 527 titles, or approximately 5.98% of the catalog, under the project's analytical rating classification.
- Older Kids represents 1,490 titles, or approximately 16.92%.

**Recommendation**

Evaluate whether additional family-oriented and children's content would address an identifiable catalog gap. Consider reviewing animation, educational children's content, and family movies as possible areas for further analysis.

**Additional Data Required**

- Family and children's content viewing hours.
- Household or profile-level audience data, where appropriately collected.
- Completion and repeat-viewing rates.
- Subscriber demand by household segment.
- Content production and licensing costs.

**Potential Business Value**

The analysis may help determine whether additional family-oriented content is justified by audience demand. The current rating distribution alone does not establish the size or profitability of the family market.

---

## 4. Analyze One-Season and Multi-Season TV Shows Separately

**Supporting Findings**

- 1,793 TV Shows have one season, approximately 67% of all TV Shows.
- 2,218 TV Shows have no more than two seasons, approximately 82.9%.
- The average number of seasons is 1.76.

**Recommendation**

Segment TV Shows into one-season and multi-season groups for further performance analysis. Evaluate whether titles with different season counts have different viewing patterns and completion outcomes. Do not assume that every one-season show is a limited series.

**Additional Data Required**

- Viewing hours by show and season.
- Season-level completion rates.
- Renewal and cancellation status.
- Production costs by season.
- Audience retention between seasons.

**Potential Business Value**

This analysis could support more informed evaluation of TV Show formats and future season decisions. The current dataset does not establish that one-season shows are cheaper, more engaging, or more successful.

---

## 5. Monitor the Balance Between Movies and TV Shows

**Supporting Findings**

- Movies represent 69.62% of the catalog.
- TV Shows represent 30.38%.

**Recommendation**

Monitor the Movie and TV Show mix as a catalog composition metric. Evaluate whether the current balance aligns with audience demand and content performance before setting any target proportion for either format.

**Additional Data Required**

- Viewing hours by content type.
- Completion rates by content type.
- Subscriber acquisition and retention associated with content types.
- Production and acquisition costs.
- Content availability and licensing duration.

**Potential Business Value**

Combining catalog share with audience and cost metrics can help assess whether the current content mix is aligned with business objectives. The available dataset does not support a specific target such as increasing TV Shows to 35–40%.

---

## 6. Use Monthly Addition Patterns as a Planning Baseline

**Supporting Findings**

- July recorded 827 content additions.
- December recorded 813 additions.
- September and October recorded 770 and 760 additions, respectively.

**Recommendation**

Use the observed monthly addition pattern as a starting point for reviewing catalog update schedules. Before prioritizing particular months for major releases, compare the pattern with audience activity, regional calendars, and release performance.

**Additional Data Required**

- Monthly viewing hours and active subscribers.
- Release-level engagement.
- Regional holidays and seasonal demand.
- Marketing campaign schedules.
- Content launch and licensing dates.

**Potential Business Value**

A combined analysis of release timing and audience activity may help evaluate content scheduling opportunities. The current data only measures when titles were added to the catalog; it does not prove seasonal viewing peaks.

---

## 7. Evaluate the Movie Duration Profile

**Supporting Findings**

- The 91–120-minute category contains 2,996 Movies, approximately 48.9% of all Movies.
- Average movie duration is approximately 99.56 minutes.
- Median movie duration is 98 minutes.

**Recommendation**

Use the 91–120-minute category as a reference point when analyzing the existing movie catalog. Compare duration groups against actual viewing and completion metrics before making decisions about preferred movie lengths or future content acquisition.

**Additional Data Required**

- Viewing hours by movie.
- Completion rates by duration group.
- Audience drop-off points.
- Genre and duration combinations.
- Production and acquisition costs by duration.

**Potential Business Value**

The combined analysis could reveal whether certain duration ranges are associated with stronger viewing outcomes. Catalog frequency alone does not establish an ideal or preferred movie duration.

---

## 8. Assess Geographic Concentration and Representation

**Supporting Findings**

- The United States contributes 3,690 titles.
- India contributes 1,046 titles.
- The catalog includes titles associated with 122 countries.

**Recommendation**

Monitor the geographic composition of the catalog and identify opportunities to improve representation where supported by audience demand, licensing availability, and strategic priorities. Because titles may be associated with multiple countries, use distinct title counts carefully when comparing country contributions.

**Additional Data Required**

- Unique title and co-production information by country.
- Regional subscriber and viewing data.
- Regional content costs.
- Licensing rights and availability.
- Audience engagement with local and international titles.

**Potential Business Value**

A clearer understanding of geographic representation and regional performance may support more informed catalog diversification decisions.

## 9. Prioritization Framework

The following prioritization is a proposed analytical workflow, not a measured estimate of financial impact.

| Priority | Recommendation | Reason for Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| High | International content diversification | The catalog has broad country representation and a high contribution from the United States. |
| High | Genre representation and content gaps | The catalog has clear concentration in several genre categories. |
| High | TV Show season analysis | One-season and two-season titles account for a large share of TV Shows. |
| Medium | Movie and TV Show balance | The catalog is strongly weighted toward Movies. |
| Medium | Family and Kids content opportunities | Little Kids has a smaller share under the analytical rating grouping. |
| Medium | Monthly addition planning | Addition counts vary across months. |
| Medium | Movie duration evaluation | The 91–120-minute group is the largest movie duration category. |
| Medium | Geographic concentration monitoring | Country contribution is uneven, with the United States ranked first. |

---

## 10. Final Recommendation Summary

The Netflix catalog analysis identifies several areas worth further investigation:

1. International content representation.
2. Genre composition and potential content gaps.
3. Family and children's content.
4. TV Show season distribution.
5. Movie and TV Show balance.
6. Monthly catalog additions.
7. Movie duration distribution.
8. Geographic representation.

The next step is to combine catalog metadata with audience engagement, subscriber, revenue, and content cost data. This would allow the recommendations to be tested against measurable business outcomes rather than relying on catalog size alone.

**Important Limitation:** The recommendations in this report are analytical proposals based on the available dataset. They do not represent confirmed Netflix policies, internal decisions, or proven causes of business performance.
